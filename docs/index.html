The menu

Everything lives in one menu.

There's no window and no settings screen. Click the icon in your menu bar and pick how long. Here's the menu while a one hour timer is running.

  1. StatusSays whether your Mac is being kept awake, how long is left and the time it ends. When Wakeintosh is off it reads "Off — Mac can sleep normally".
  2. Turn OffOnly shows up while Wakeintosh is on. Your Mac goes back to sleeping the way it normally does.
  3. Keep Awake ForIndefinitely stays on until you turn it off. The others count down from 15 minutes up to 8 hours, then turn off by themselves. Picking another one replaces the current timer.
  4. Custom…Type any number of minutes. Wakeintosh remembers the last number you used, and starts at 90 the first time.
  5. Allow Display to SleepThe screen can turn off as usual while the Mac itself keeps working. Good for downloads, backups and long builds. Changing it mid-timer keeps the time you have left.
  6. Open at LoginTurned on the first time you open Wakeintosh, so it's in your menu bar after a restart. One click turns it off.
  7. Quit WakeintoshQuitting also stops keeping your Mac awake. There's nothing left running in the background.

What it does

Small on purpose.

One click to stay awake

Stops your Mac, its display and its disk from going to sleep, the same as running caffeinate -dimsu yourself.

A timer you can see

Pick 15 minutes to 8 hours, or any number of minutes. The time left counts down next to the icon, and your Mac goes back to normal at zero.

14:591:59:59

Screen off, Mac still working

Turn on Allow Display to Sleep and the screen turns off as usual, while downloads and builds keep going.

Lets go if it quits

If Wakeintosh quits or crashes, your Mac goes back to sleeping normally. It can't leave your Mac stuck awake.

The icon tells you

In colour while your Mac is being kept awake, grey when it isn't. You can tell at a glance without opening anything.

AwakeOff

Menu bar only

No Dock icon and no windows. It opens at login, sits in the menu bar and stays out of the way.

How it works

It runs Apple's own caffeinate.

macOS ships with a tool called caffeinate that holds your Mac awake. Wakeintosh starts it with the right options and stops it when you're done. Pick the options and see the exact command it runs.

The whole app is one Swift file, main.swift. Read it in a couple of minutes.

  • -dStops the display from sleeping.Left out when Allow Display to Sleep is on.
  • -iStops the Mac from going to sleep when it's idle.
  • -mStops the disk from going to sleep when it's idle.
  • -sStops the system from sleeping.macOS only honours this one on AC power.
  • -uTells macOS someone is using the Mac.
  • -wWatches Wakeintosh's process ID. When the app quits or crashes, caffeinate exits too.
  • -tThe timer, in seconds. caffeinate lets go when it runs out.Not used for Indefinitely.

Install

Two minutes to set up.

$brew install --cask rishikeshsreekumar/tap/wakeintosh

Then open Wakeintosh from Applications or Spotlight and look for the icon in your menu bar. Homebrew skips the macOS "can't verify" prompt for you.

Update later with brew upgrade --cask wakeintosh. Remove it with brew uninstall --cask wakeintosh, and add --zap to remove its settings too.

  1. Download Wakeintosh-1.0.dmg from the latest release.
  2. Open it and drag Wakeintosh onto Applications.
  3. Open Wakeintosh from Applications.
"Wakeintosh can't be opened"? It isn't signed with a paid Apple Developer certificate yet, so macOS asks you to confirm the first time. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. Or run this once in Terminal:
$xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Wakeintosh.app

Needs the Xcode Command Line Tools (xcode-select --install).

$git clone https://github.com/RishikeshSreekumar/wakeintosh.git
$cd wakeintosh && ./build.sh --install

build.sh --install builds a universal app, copies it to Applications and opens it.

FAQ

Questions people ask

macOS says it "can't verify" Wakeintosh. Is that normal?

Yes. Wakeintosh isn't notarized with a paid Apple Developer certificate yet, so macOS asks once. Open System Settings, go to Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. Or run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Wakeintosh.app in Terminal. Homebrew installs skip this step.

Does it change my sleep settings?

No. It doesn't touch System Settings. While it's on, it holds your Mac awake through caffeinate. When you turn it off, the timer runs out or you quit, your Mac goes back to the settings you already had.

What if Wakeintosh crashes?

Your Mac goes back to sleeping normally. Wakeintosh starts caffeinate with -w and its own process ID, so caffeinate exits as soon as the app does, however it ends.

Why not just run caffeinate in Terminal?

You can, and that's all Wakeintosh does underneath. It saves you the Terminal window, shows the time left in the menu bar, lets you turn the screen-sleep option on and off without starting over, and comes back after a restart.

Does it work on battery?

It stops idle sleep on battery too. One of the options it passes, -s, only counts on AC power, because that's how Apple's caffeinate works.

How do I stop it opening at login?

Click the icon and untick Open at Login. You can also remove it under System Settings → General → Login Items.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac on macOS 13 Ventura or later. It's a universal app, so it runs natively on Apple Silicon and Intel.

How do I uninstall it?

With Homebrew, run brew uninstall --cask wakeintosh (add --zap to remove its settings). Otherwise quit Wakeintosh from its menu and move it from Applications to the Bin.

Can I read the code?

Yes. It's on GitHub under the MIT license. The app is a single Swift file, and the icon is drawn in code too.
